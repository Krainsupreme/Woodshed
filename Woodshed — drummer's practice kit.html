The 40 rudiments

The Percussive Arts Society's International Drum Rudiments, grouped as PAS groups them. Sticking is shown where it's unambiguous in plain text; lowercase means a grace note (flam or drag).

Audio is a synthesized snare: right hand panned slightly right, left hand slightly left, accents louder, grace notes soft. Each example is one common interpretation of the rhythm, not the official notation. Roll rudiments in particular are played several ways (open, measured, or in different subdivisions). Rudiments marked "No audio" are left out because their rhythm or accent placement couldn't be encoded with confidence. The official PAS chart, linked below, is the authority.

    The shelf

    Method books drum teachers have assigned for decades, roughly in the order most players meet them.

    Stick Control

    George Lawrence Stone, 1935

    Pages of single-line sticking permutations. The default hands book; play every line to a click.

    Syncopation

    Ted Reed, 1958

    Progressive reading exercises. Teachers reuse its pages for dozens of applications (comping, fills, independence).

    Accents and Rebounds

    George Lawrence Stone, 1961

    The follow-up to Stick Control, focused on accent control and rebound.

    Advanced Techniques for the Modern Drummer

    Jim Chapin, 1948

    Jazz coordination: steady ride pattern against independent snare and bass drum lines.

    The New Breed

    Gary Chester, 1985

    Demanding four-limb independence system built around reading over ostinatos, with singing parts.

    Future Sounds

    David Garibaldi, 1990

    Linear funk patterns from the Tower of Power drummer.

    Groove Essentials

    Tommy Igoe

    Play-along charts covering a wide range of styles, with tempo-graded tracks.

    Links

    Organizations and long-running sites. Check them yourself; site structure changes.

    • Percussive Arts Society — publishes the official 40 rudiments chart.
    • Modern Drummer — the long-running drum magazine and its archive.
    • Drumeo — large lesson library; mostly paid, with free YouTube content.
    • Melodics — practice app that scores your timing as you play: e-kits over MIDI, acoustic kits and practice pads through the mic. Some lessons free; the full library is a subscription.
    • Vic Firth — stick maker with free educational material.
    • r/drums — community Q&A; quality varies.
    • mobygratis — Moby's free instrumental library, many tracks with multitrack stems; useful for play-along practice. Free for non-commercial use; check the license before using it in released work.
    • Open Close Hand Technique — YouTube video lesson on open-close technique: opening and closing the hand around the stick to work with the rebound rather than against it.
    Use hearing protection around acoustic kits. Sustained drum-kit levels commonly exceed the exposure limits hearing-safety agencies recommend.
